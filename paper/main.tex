\title{Does the Model Know It Was Wrong,\\or Just What It Said?}
\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reading a model's own errors out of its hidden states is now a standard technique, used for
hallucination detection, error prediction and selective prediction, and routinely reported as an
AUROC against a reference of $0.5$. We show that this estimator has several failure modes, and that
the conventional controls do not detect them.

Three inflate the reported value. The reference point is wrong: an observer with no self-knowledge
whatever scores far above $0.5$ on this metric, and higher as it becomes more accurate, so the
comparison credits first-order accuracy. Correctness is agreement between an output and a label, so
within a set of items sharing a label, decoding the model's \emph{own output} is decoding
correctness --- a channel that conditioning on the label does not close, and that any probe read
after generation is exposed to. And item difficulty enters wherever positive and negative items are
not the same text. Two further failures are properties of the nested comparison used to isolate what
hidden states add to a behavioural baseline, which breaks in opposite directions depending on its
regularisation, once discarding real signal and once discarding the added block; both pass a
conventional planted-data check.

We give two reference distributions to replace $0.5$, an estimator whose acceptance criterion is
recovery of a planted effect across magnitudes rather than at one, and a detection threshold below
which a null is uninformative irrespective of its confidence interval. We demonstrate on LLM judges,
where both leakage channels are maximal because the output is a single token compared directly to
the label, and measure output decoding at \VerdictDecodHi{}. The failures are properties of the
correctness target rather than of judges, and \cref{sec:generalisation} maps which apply to which
existing setups.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LLM judges score model outputs at a scale that precludes human review, so a signal that identifies
unreliable verdicts has value. The standard proposal reads it from the judge: fit a probe on the
judge's internal state to predict whether its verdict was correct, and report the AUROC. The
construction is cheap and returns values between $0.6$ and $0.9$.

The quantity it returns is not metacognitive sensitivity. One instance makes the problem concrete
before any formalism. Correctness is defined as agreement between the judge's verdict and the label,
so \emph{within a set of items sharing a label}, ``was the judge right'' and ``what did the judge
say'' are the same question. A probe that reads the judge's own verdict off its final hidden state
therefore scores perfectly on that set while carrying no information about reliability --- and the
final hidden state is exactly where the verdict is read from, so this is not a hypothetical: we
measure such decoding at \VerdictDecodHi{}. Conditioning on the label, the standard remedy for the
better-known confound, does not remove it, because the quantity being decoded is the judge's output
rather than the label.

\Cref{fig:sdt} shows the second instance. An observer with no metacognition at all --- one latent
decision variable, a threshold, and no access to its own errors --- scores far above $0.5$ on this
metric, and higher as it becomes more accurate. Reporting against $0.5$ therefore credits a judge's
first-order accuracy as though it were self-knowledge.

This paper characterises the resulting estimator and supplies the corrections.

Five properties separate the reported quantity from the intended one. Two are leakage channels, one
through the ground-truth label and one through the judge's own verdict, requiring different
corrections (\cref{sec:leakage}). Two are failures of the nested comparison that is supposed to
measure what internals add to behaviour, and they act in opposite directions: concatenation under a
shared penalty removes signal from the baseline, and an offset formulation with cross-validated
ridge removes the added block (\cref{sec:ladder}). The fifth concerns the reference point, which is
conventionally $0.5$ and should be the performance of an observer that decides as well as this judge
but has no access to how it decided (\cref{sec:estimand}).

Four of the five inflate the reported value and one deflates it. The conclusion is not that internal
signals are weaker than claimed but that the published quantity differs from the intended one.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Two reference distributions for type-2 AUROC in judge evaluation}, replacing $0.5$: a
      first-order signal-detection floor, which is a Monte-Carlo form of meta-$d'$/$d'$, and a
      verdict-leakage floor arising from incomplete sign cancellation at unbalanced strata
      (\cref{sec:estimand}).
\item \textbf{A two-dimensional leakage taxonomy} for the correctness target, distinguishing
      item-identity leakage, removed by conditioning, from self-verdict leakage, removed only by
      pooled estimation (\cref{sec:leakage}).
\item \textbf{Two opposed failure modes of nested estimation from wide representations}, with a
      corrected estimator and an acceptance criterion --- recovery of a planted effect across
      magnitudes --- that both failures violate (\cref{sec:ladder}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Metacognition and the type-2 ROC.} Separating an observer's first-order sensitivity from
its insight into its own performance has an established methodology. Meta-$d'$ \citep{maniscalco2012}
expresses metacognitive sensitivity in the units of first-order $d'$, and the ratio meta-$d'$/$d'$
is the conventional normalisation for first-order performance \citep{fleming2014}. A type-2 ROC
computed against $0.5$ conflates the two: a more sensitive first-order observer scores higher on it
without metacognition. That tool has been applied to language models; the contribution here is to
connect it to the interpretability literature, which reports type-2 AUROC against $0.5$, and to
measure what the omission costs in a setting where two additional leakage channels are present.

\paragraph{Probing and its controls.} That a probe's accuracy reflects the probe rather than the
representation motivates the control tasks of \citet{hewitt2019control}. The subsequent literature
pursues the concern in opposing directions, \citet{voita2020} recasting probing as compression and
\citet{pimentel2020} arguing from mutual information for the highest-performing probe rather than
the simplest, and separately through whether a model uses what a probe can read
\citep{ravichander2021,elazar2021}, synthesised by \citet{belinkov2022}. The positive control of
\cref{sec:absence} follows that tradition. The failures in \cref{sec:ladder} concern nested model
comparison under disparate column counts rather than probe capacity; we are not aware of a prior
report of either.

\paragraph{Judge evaluation.} Judges are evaluated on purpose-built benchmarks
\citep{zheng2023,tan2024,zeng2024}, whose documented pathologies include position, verbosity and
self-enhancement bias \citep{zheng2023}. The two free-text banks used here differ in label
semantics: \citet{tan2024} labels responses by objective correctness rather than human preference,
and \citet{zeng2024} is adversarial by construction, its 419 curated pairs pairing an
instruction-violating response that is superficially attractive against a compliant one.

\paragraph{Selective prediction.} The applied form of the question is abstention: the precision
available at a fixed coverage \citep{elyaniv2010,geifman2017}. We report coverage at fixed risk
rather than $\Delta$AUROC for the applied claim.

\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Judging.} A judge scores an item in one forward pass. The verdict is read from the
next-token log-probabilities of two verdict tokens at the position following \texttt{Answer:}.
Formats are \texttt{mcq} and \texttt{single} (Yes/No) and \texttt{pairwise} (A/B, presented in both
orders). Prompts are raw and identical across the panel; a chat-template ablation moves a competent
judge by at most $1.5$ points.

\paragraph{Notation.} For item $i \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ let $g_i \in \mathcal{V}$ be the ground-truth
verdict, $\hat{v}_i \in \mathcal{V}$ the judge's verdict, and $|\mathcal{V}| = 2$. The target is
\begin{equation}
y_i \;=\; \mathbb{1}[\hat{v}_i = g_i],
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
and the \emph{stratum} of item $i$ is $g_i$. The margin $m_i = \ell_i(v_1) - \ell_i(v_2)$ is the
difference of the two verdict log-probabilities, so $\hat{v}_i = v_1 \iff m_i > 0$: the verdict is a
deterministic function of $\operatorname{sign}(m_i)$. Feature blocks are $X^{\mathrm{b}}$ (baseline:
margin, $|m|$, $\hat v$, prompt length, task-start index, subject, peer difficulty),
$X^{\mathrm{s}}$ (spectral) and $X^{\mathrm{a}}$ (activations), with
$d_{\mathrm{a}} = \dim X^{\mathrm{a}}$ the model's hidden size. Cross-validation groups $q_i$ are
derived from normalised question text, so that the two orders of a pairwise item and the
positive/negative partners of one question share a fold.

\paragraph{Banks.} Five banks (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, JudgeBench, LLMBar, RewardBench~2), balanced $50/50$
by construction, seeded, byte-identical across judges. Grouping on text rather than identifier
collapses 419 LLMBar pairs into 418 groups, one instruction appearing verbatim in two adversarial
subsets, and 620 JudgeBench identifiers into 528 groups.

\paragraph{Features.} Three families in increasing cost. Margin: $m$ and $|m|$. Activation: the
last-token hidden state at half and full depth. Spectral: attention-graph diagnostics --- Fiedler
value, high-frequency energy ratio, smoothness, spectral entropy, and a Fiedler-velocity summary ---
on the symmetric normalised Laplacian over a task-token subgraph, summarised as mean, slope and
normalised argmax over the last third of layers. The summary is fixed \emph{a priori} at three
numbers per metric. Precision is \texttt{bfloat16} uniformly (\cref{sec:hygiene}).

\section{Reference distributions for type-2 AUROC}
\label{sec:estimand}

\subsection{The conditional estimand}

The headline metric compares items only within a stratum:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AUROC}_{\mathrm{c}}(s) \;=\;
\Pr\!\big[\, s_i > s_j \;\big|\; y_i = 1,\; y_j = 0,\; g_i = g_j \,\big],
\label{eq:cond}
\end{equation}
estimated as a discordant-pair-weighted average over strata. Within a stratum the ground-truth label
is constant, which removes the first leakage channel (\cref{sec:identity}). Strata contributing
fewer than five items of either class are excluded from the estimate.

\subsection{The first-order floor}

Let a first-order observer hold a latent decision variable $s_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{g_i}, 1)$ with
$\mu_{v_1} - \mu_{v_2} = d'$, emit $\hat v_i = v_1 \iff s_i > c$, and report margin $m_i = s_i - c$.

\begin{definition}[First-order floor]
\label{def:sdt}
$F_{\mathrm{SDT}}(d', c)$ is the distribution of $\mathrm{AUROC}_{\mathrm{c}}$ obtained by applying
the estimator of \cref{eq:cond} to data generated by the observer above, with $d'$ and $c$ estimated
from the judge's own hit and false-alarm rates and re-estimated on each bootstrap resample.
\end{definition}

This observer has no access to its own errors beyond $\operatorname{sign}(m_i)$, and
\cref{tab:sdt} gives its conditional AUROC. \Cref{def:sdt} is the Monte-Carlo form of
meta-$d'$/$d'$ \citep{maniscalco2012,fleming2014}. Each rung is reported as a percentile against
$F_{\mathrm{SDT}}$, and those percentiles enter the same Benjamini--Hochberg family as every other
contrast.

\subsection{The verdict-leakage floor}
\label{sec:verdictfloor}

Within the stratum $g = v_1$, item $i$ is correct iff $\hat v_i = v_1$. A feature measurable with
respect to $\hat v$ alone is therefore a perfect within-stratum predictor.

\begin{definition}[Verdict-leakage floor]
\label{def:leak}
$F_{\mathrm{V}}$ is the distribution of $\mathrm{AUROC}_{\mathrm{c}}$ attained by a feature block
$\sigma(\hat v)$ that is a deterministic function of the judge's verdict and independent of
everything else, evaluated at the slice's observed stratum imbalance under the same pooled
estimator.
\end{definition}

A pooled fit cannot select such a feature, because it scores $1$ in one stratum and $0$ in the
other. That cancellation is exact only at balanced strata, and judges are biased: verdict bias
spans $0.14$ to $0.64$ across our panel. $F_{\mathrm{V}}$ measures the residual. A rung must clear
\begin{equation}
\phi \;=\; \max\!\big(\, Q_{95}(F_{\mathrm{SDT}}),\; Q_{95}(F_{\mathrm{V}}) \,\big),
\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
which on our panel ranges over \FloorLo{}--\FloorHi{}.

\section{Two leakage channels}
\label{sec:leakage}

\subsection{Item identity}
\label{sec:identity}

Because $y_i$ is determined by $\hat v_i$ and $g_i$ jointly and every judge has verdict bias, $y$ is
largely determined by $g$ alone. Any feature separating a positive from a negative item scores
without carrying information about the judge's reliability, and text-derived features do so
trivially. The \texttt{identity\_decodability} control reports how well the same features predict
$g$; it runs \IdentLo{}--\IdentHi{} across slices and is invariant across judges of very different
competence, making it a property of the benchmark rather than of the model under test.
Conditioning on $g$ removes it.

\subsection{Self-verdict}
\label{sec:verdict}

The second channel survives conditioning, because the decoded quantity is $\hat v$ rather than $g$
(\cref{def:leak}). The last-token hidden state decodes $\hat v$ at
\VerdictDecodLo{}--\VerdictDecodHi{}, which is expected: it is the state from which the verdict
logit is read.

Protection comes from the pooled fit, a property of the estimation rather than of the metric.
Fitting within stratum removes it. On planted data in which a feature decodes $\hat v$ and nothing
else, a pooled fit attains approximately $0.5$ and a within-stratum fit exceeds
\PooledFitGuardWithin{}. This is enforced by a regression test.

Leakage into type-2 AUROC is thus two-dimensional: item identity, removed by conditioning, and
self-verdict, removed only by pooled estimation at balanced strata.

\section{Nested estimation from wide representations}
\label{sec:ladder}

A nested comparison estimates what a block adds to a baseline. We report two failures of that
estimate, acting in opposite directions, for blocks whose column count exceeds the baseline's by
three orders of magnitude.

\subsection{Concatenation under a shared penalty}

Fitting one penalised model on $[X^{\mathrm{b}}, X^{\mathrm{a}}]$ with $d_{\mathrm{a}} \gg
\dim X^{\mathrm{b}}$ does not estimate an increment: the shared penalty admits a solution
approximating $X^{\mathrm{a}}$ alone, requiring the block to reconstruct the margin. \Cref{tab:swamp}
gives the effect with a \emph{pure-noise} block, for which the correct delta is zero at every width.
The damage increases with baseline strength, and across the six estimable slices of our panel the
delta correlates with baseline strength at $r = -0.85$.

\subsection{The offset estimator, and its own failure mode}
\label{sec:offset}

Let $z$ be the baseline's out-of-fold logit. The offset estimator scores
\begin{equation}
\hat s \;=\; z + f(X^{\mathrm{a}}), \qquad
f = \arg\min_{w} \; \sum_i \log\!\big(1 + e^{-(2y_i-1)(z_i + X^{\mathrm{a}}_i w)}\big)
      \;+\; \alpha \lVert w \rVert^2 ,
\label{eq:offset}
\end{equation}
with $z$ entering unpenalised. Addition is then monotone up to noise: an uninformative block drives
$f \to 0$ and $\hat s \to z$. Two conditions are load-bearing. The offset for training rows must
come from an inner cross-validation within the training fold, or the outer test fold enters the
baseline the second stage observes. And $\alpha$ must be selected by out-of-fold AUROC.

Selecting $\alpha$ instead by squared error on the working residual $y - \sigma(z)$ --- the natural
ridge formulation --- fails. That objective is on a different scale from the logit, its
cross-validation curve is nearly flat at $n = \PilotSliceN{}$ with $d_{\mathrm{a}} = 1536$, and its
minimum lies at maximum shrinkage: the selected penalty reached the top of the grid on every fold,
the resulting correction had \OffsetShrinkRatio{} of the offset's scale, and every internal rung
returned a value numerically equal to the baseline. Capping the grid does not address this; it
selects the penalty by hand.

\subsection{Acceptance criterion}
\label{sec:absence}

Both failures passed a planted test at a single signal magnitude. The criterion is therefore a
\emph{recovery curve}: a planted signal along a dense random direction at several magnitudes, with
the requirement that zero signal costs approximately nothing and a recoverable signal is expressed.
A single-column plant understates the estimator and is not used.

The curve yields a \emph{detection threshold}, below which the comparison is uninformative
irrespective of its standard error. At width \RecoveryWidth{} with \RecoverySeeds{} paired seeds per
cell, detection reaches \DetectFracFourHundred{} of seeds at $n = 400$ and \DetectFracTwoHundred{} at
$n = \PilotSliceN{}$, giving a preregistered $n = \NFreeze{}$ per stratum. Two further diagnostics
distinguish absence from redundancy and from shrinkage: block-only rungs, fitted without baseline
columns; and a positive control recovering a target known to be encoded, which attains at least
\PosControlLo{} on every slice, establishing that a null reflects the signal rather than the
pipeline.

\section{Which failures apply where}
\label{sec:generalisation}

Every failure above is a property of the target $y = \mathbb{1}[\hat v = g]$, not of judges. Judges
are the sharpest demonstration because the output is a single token compared directly to the label,
which makes both leakage channels maximal, but the scope is wider. \Cref{tab:generalisation} maps
them onto the settings this estimator is used in.

The first row is the most portable. It applies to hidden-state correctness probes
\citep{azaria2023} and to behavioural self-evaluation \citep{kadavath2022} alike, since it concerns
the reference the number is compared against rather than the feature family. The second is the
sharpest: a probe read after generation shares half its target with the thing being predicted. The
third is partially controlled in existing work --- \citet{azaria2023} note that a model's own
sentence probability tracks sentence length and word frequency, and prefer a trained classifier for
that reason, which is a control for the same nuisance channel our baseline carries.

\section{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

\paragraph{Clustering.} Items are clustered by construction: the two orders of a pairwise item and
the positive/negative partners of one question share a group. Paired DeLong assumes independence.
On planted data with one item per group its standard error matches the grouped bootstrap
(\DelongSEIndep{} for both); with four clustered rows per question it understates the standard
error by \DelongRatioClustered{}$\times$. We use a grouped paired bootstrap, which is itself paired,
and report DeLong as a labelled cross-check.

\paragraph{Equivalence.} Each contrast carries a two-one-sided-test verdict against a band of
\EqBand{} conditional AUROC, read from the same bootstrap.

\paragraph{Power, and its limits.} At \PilotSliceN{} items per slice and a median standard error of
\MedianSE{}, the minimum detectable effect is \MdeUncorrected{} uncorrected and \MdeFDR{} under
Benjamini--Hochberg over \FamilySize{} contrasts. This statistic is not sufficient on its own: it
measures the precision of a difference between two score vectors, and a collapsed estimator returns
a difference of zero with a standard error to match. The detection threshold of \cref{sec:absence}
is the primary criterion, and the minimum detectable effect is retained as a lower bound.

\caption{Per-slice estimation}
\label{alg:pipeline}

  \State fit baseline on $X^{\mathrm{b}}$; $z_{\text{test}} \gets$ its out-of-fold logit
  \State $z_{\text{train}} \gets$ inner-CV logit within the training fold

\EndFor
\State report $\mathrm{AUROC}_{\mathrm{c}}$ (\cref{eq:cond}), its percentile against
       $F_{\mathrm{SDT}}$, the block-only rung, and per-stratum support
\State grouped paired bootstrap for each contrast; TOST against \EqBand{}; BH-FDR over the family
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Two panels are reported, and they are distinct experiments. The \emph{pilot panel} is four judges
below 4B at \PilotSliceN{} items per slice on one bank, and it is complete; it establishes the
estimator's behaviour and the size of both floors. The \emph{main panel} is
\NJudgesMain{} judges spanning scale at \NItemsPerSliceMain{} items per stratum across all five
banks, and it carries the preregistered primary tests. Results below are labelled by panel and no
quantity is pooled across them.

\subsection{Pilot panel}

\paragraph{Spectral features alone.} Fitted without baseline columns, the spectral family attained
\MTwoOnlyLo{}--\MTwoOnlyHi{} conditional AUROC across \NSlicesEstimable{} estimable slices, at or
below chance on every one. The activation family attained \MThreeOnlyLo{}--\MThreeOnlyHi{}. Both sit
at or below the estimator's detection threshold at this $n$ (\cref{sec:absence}), so the nested
comparison between them is uninformative on this panel and is reported as unresolved rather than as
an equivalence.

\paragraph{Floors and controls.} The floor $\phi$ of \cref{eq:floor} ranged over
\FloorLo{}--\FloorHi{}, exceeding every rung on all but one slice. The positive control attained at
least \PosControlLo{} on every slice, establishing that a null on this panel reflects the signal
rather than the pipeline.

\section{Numerical and provenance conditions}
\label{sec:hygiene}

\paragraph{Precision.} Under \texttt{float16} one judge produced non-finite attention weights. Its
spectral analysis failed on every item, and its verdict logits were corrupted: it presented as
degenerate (\NumFpAcc{} accuracy, \NumFpBias{} verdict bias) where under \texttt{bfloat16} it is
competent (\NumBfAcc{}). The two precisions agree on \NumAgreeFpBf{} of its verdicts. Precision must
be uniform across a panel, since neither spectral metrics nor logits are comparable across dtypes.
The analysis library now raises on the first layer of non-finite attention.

\paragraph{Versions.} Spectral rows computed under different library versions are not comparable,
and the version appears only in the run log. We verified that the change on the code path in use
shifts every metric by at most \VerDtypeShift{}, and pin the library by commit rather than by
version string.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Chain-of-thought.} The judges here are single-token log-probability readouts, which is what
production reward pipelines use but not the only deployment. \Cref{def:sdt} transfers unchanged,
requiring only a two-way argmax over a scalar margin at the readout token, but generated chain
length correlates with item difficulty and would enter the baseline as a nuisance covariate. We
expect internals to add less under chain-of-thought, since the model can externalise uncertainty
into tokens the readout state has already absorbed.

\textbf{Panel.} Internal access requires open weights.

\textbf{Label semantics.} On preference datasets the \texttt{single} format treats a relative
preference as absolute truth, capping achievable accuracy. Pairwise is primary, and is additionally
immune to the length confound by counterbalancing.

\textbf{Spectral summarisation.} Three numbers per metric, fixed \emph{a priori}. If the signal is
layer-local this design cannot observe it, and a null would concern the summarisation.

\textbf{Recovery-curve indexing.} We index the recovery curve on planted strength rather than on a
recovered block-only AUROC, because indexing on an estimated quantity is ill-posed: the recovered
value carries the same shrinkage that suppresses the delta. At the width and sample size measured
here that bias is negligible, so on this measurement the benefit came from pairing and seeding the
cells. The bias is a function of width and sample size, and the plant-indexed form remains the
recommendation.
