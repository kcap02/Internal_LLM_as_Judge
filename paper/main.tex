\title{How Not to Measure Whether a Judge Knows It Is Wrong}
\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{abstract}
Asking whether an LLM judge's internal state predicts its own errors is a type-2 (correctness) ROC
problem, and the standard way of computing it is wrong in five separable ways. We measure each.
First, because a judge is correct exactly when its verdict matches the label, any feature that
merely separates positive from negative items scores well while carrying no self-knowledge;
identity decodability runs \IdentLo{}--\IdentHi{} on our benchmark irrespective of which model
judges. Second, and not removed by conditioning on the label, within a label stratum \emph{correct}
means \emph{the judge said this stratum}, so a representation decoding the judge's own verdict is a
perfect within-stratum predictor --- and the last-token hidden state decodes it at
\VerdictDecodPlanted{}. What blocks this is the pooled fit, not the metric, and only when the strata
are balanced. Third, concatenating a hidden-state block onto a small baseline under a shared penalty
does not measure increment: a block of \AddBlockWide{} \emph{pure-noise} columns costs a strong
baseline \SwampLossWide{} AUROC, and the damage grows with baseline strength. Fourth, the
field-default paired DeLong test understates the standard error by \DelongRatioClustered{}$\times$ on
pairwise judge data, which is clustered by construction. Fifth, the reference point is not $0.5$: a
first-order signal-detection observer with no metacognition whatever reaches \SdtAurocMid{} at
accuracy \SdtAccMid{}, so type-2 AUROC scored against $0.5$ credits first-order accuracy as
self-knowledge. We give a corrected estimator for each, with a regression test apiece, and report a
demonstration panel of confidence-margin, activation-probe and attention-graph spectral features
under it. The one result that survives every correction unchanged is an equivalence: spectral
features add nothing beyond a linear activation probe, with deltas in
\AnchorDeltaLo{}--\AnchorDeltaHi{} at standard errors of \AnchorSELo{}--\AnchorSEHi{}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LLM judges are used to score model outputs at a scale that forbids human review, so knowing when a
judge is wrong is worth something. A natural proposal is to read it off the judge itself: train a
probe on the judge's internal state to predict whether its verdict was correct, and report the AUROC.
The construction is standard, it is cheap, and it produces numbers between $0.6$ and $0.9$ with
reassuring regularity.

Those numbers are hard to interpret, for a reason visible in the target variable. Correctness is
defined as $\texttt{is\_correct} = (\texttt{pred} = \texttt{gt})$, a comparison of two things, and a
probe can score well by knowing about either one without knowing anything about the judge's own
reliability. This paper takes that observation seriously and follows it through the estimator.

We find five separable failures. Two are leakage channels: one through the label, one through the
judge's own verdict, and they require different fixes (\cref{sec:leakage}). One is in the ladder
that is supposed to measure what internals \emph{add} to behaviour, which under a shared penalty
measures something else entirely and does so in a direction that makes internals look
\emph{worse} than they are (\cref{sec:ladder}). One is in the inference, where the field-default
paired test assumes an independence that pairwise judge benchmarks violate by construction
(\cref{sec:inference}). And one is in the reference point, where $0.5$ implies a comparison to a
judge that cannot decide anything, rather than to a judge that decides as well as this one but knows
nothing about how it decided (\cref{sec:estimand}).

Four of the five inflate the reported number and one deflates it, which is worth stating plainly:
this is not an argument that internal signals are weaker than claimed, it is an argument that the
published quantity is not the one anybody wants.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We identify the type-2 estimand for judge metacognition and give it two floors rather than one:
a first-order signal-detection floor, which is the Monte-Carlo form of meta-$d'$/$d'$, and a
verdict-leakage floor arising from incomplete sign cancellation at unbalanced strata.
(ii) We give the two-dimensional leakage taxonomy --- item identity and self-verdict --- and show
that the second is blocked by the pooled fit rather than by the conditional metric, making it
fragile to a refactor that looks innocuous.
(iii) We show that the usual nested-model ladder does not measure increment at realistic block
width, quantify the damage with a pure-noise block, and give a monotone offset estimator.
(iv) We quantify the clustering penalty for paired DeLong on pairwise judge data.
(v) We report a demonstration panel under the corrected estimator, and one equivalence result that
survives all five corrections.
(vi) We report our own analytic errors grouped by cause (\cref{sec:corrections}), including two that
were about to enter a write-up as findings.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Metacognition and the type-2 ROC.} Distinguishing an observer's first-order sensitivity
from its insight into its own performance is the subject of a long methodological literature.
Meta-$d'$ \citep{maniscalco2012} expresses metacognitive sensitivity in the units of first-order
$d'$, and the ratio meta-$d'$/$d'$ --- the M-ratio --- is $1.0$ when an observer has no insight
beyond what its first-order performance already implies \citep{fleming2014}. The essential point,
which the probing literature on LLMs has not imported, is that a type-2 ROC computed against $0.5$
is uninterpretable: a more accurate first-order observer scores higher on it without any
metacognition at all. \Cref{sec:estimand} is a Monte-Carlo M-ratio for judges.

\paragraph{Probing and its controls.} That a probe's score reflects the probe rather than the
representation is the concern of \citet{hewitt2019control}, answered with control tasks; the
follow-on literature sharpens it through probe capacity \citep{voita2020,pimentel2020}, through
whether the model uses the information \citep{ravichander2021,elazar2021}, and in the synthesis of
\citet{belinkov2022}. Our positive control (\cref{sec:absence}) is in that tradition. The failure in
\cref{sec:ladder} is different in kind and, as far as we know, undocumented: it is not about probe
capacity but about what happens to a \emph{nested} comparison when the two models being compared
have very different column counts under one penalty.

\paragraph{LLM judges.} Judge models are evaluated on benchmarks built for the purpose
\citep{zheng2023,lambert2024,tan2024,zeng2024}, whose known pathologies --- position bias, length
bias, self-preference --- motivate the confound register we carry alongside this work. Our judging
setup is logprob readout, which is what reward models and constrained-decoding judge pipelines
actually do at scale.

\paragraph{Selective prediction.} The practical form of the question is abstention: given a signal,
what precision is available at a fixed coverage \citep{elyaniv2010,geifman2017}. We report
coverage-at-fixed-risk rather than $\Delta$AUROC for the applied claim, because a $0.02$ AUROC
difference has no operational meaning and a coverage change does.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Judging.} A judge scores an item in one forward pass; the verdict is read from the
next-token logprobs of two verdict tokens at the position following \texttt{Answer:}. The
\emph{margin} is the signed difference of those two logprobs, so the predicted verdict is a
deterministic function of $\mathrm{sign}(\text{margin})$ by construction --- a fact that matters in
\cref{sec:leakage,sec:estimand}. Three formats: \texttt{mcq} and \texttt{single} (Yes/No) and
\texttt{pairwise} (A/B, presented in \emph{both} orders). Prompts are raw and identical across the
panel; a chat-template ablation moves a competent judge by at most $1.5$ points.

\paragraph{Banks.} Five banks (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, JudgeBench, LLMBar, RewardBench~2), balanced 50/50 by
construction, seeded, and byte-identical across judges. Cross-validation groups are derived from
normalised question \emph{text}, not identifiers: JudgeBench reuses 92 questions across its two
splits, and grouping on identifiers would place the same question in train and test.

\paragraph{Features.} Three families in increasing cost. \textbf{Margin}: the signed logprob margin
and its absolute value. \textbf{Activation}: the last-token hidden state at half and full depth.
\textbf{Spectral}: attention-graph diagnostics --- Fiedler value, high-frequency energy ratio,
smoothness, spectral entropy, plus a Fiedler-velocity summary --- computed on the symmetric
normalised Laplacian over a task-token subgraph, summarised as mean, slope and normalised argmax
over the last third of layers. The summary is fixed \emph{a priori}: three numbers per metric, no
post-hoc layer selection. Precision is \texttt{bfloat16} uniformly (\cref{sec:hygiene}).

\paragraph{The ladder.} Rungs are $M_n$ (nuisance only: length, task-start index, subject), $M_1$
(margin and verdict), $M_{1n}$ ($+$ nuisance), $M_{1nd}$ ($+$ leave-one-model-out peer difficulty),
$M_2$ ($+$ spectral), $M_3$ ($+$ activations), $M_4$ (everything). The baseline every internal claim
must clear is $M_{1nd}$ where at least two judges scored the same items.

\section{The estimand and its two floors}
\label{sec:estimand}

\subsection{Neither floor is 0.5}

Write $y = \texttt{is\_correct}$ and let the strata be the ground-truth verdict. The headline metric
is the \emph{conditional} AUROC: the probability that a correct item outscores an incorrect one
\emph{drawn from the same stratum}. Within a stratum the item's label is constant, which removes the
first leakage channel (\cref{sec:identity}).

It does not follow that $0.5$ is the reference. Consider a first-order signal-detection observer: one
latent decision variable $s$, verdict $=\mathrm{sign}(s-c)$, margin $=s-c$, and nothing whatever that
knows about its own errors. Its discriminability $d'$ drives its accuracy, and it drives the
informativeness of its margin at the same time. Simulating this observer and pushing it through the
identical features, estimator and metric:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{A judge with \emph{no} metacognition, at three discriminabilities. Type-2 AUROC scored
against $0.5$ would read the third row as strong self-knowledge.}
\label{tab:sdt}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
& accuracy & $M_1$ conditional AUROC \\
\midrule
$d' \approx 0$   & \SdtAccLow{}  & \SdtAurocLow{}  \\
$d' = 0.9$       & \SdtAccMid{}  & \SdtAurocMid{}  \\
$d' = 1.4$       & \SdtAccHigh{} & \SdtAurocHigh{} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This is the M-ratio of \citet{maniscalco2012} in Monte-Carlo form. We estimate each judge's own $d'$
and criterion from its hit and false-alarm rates, simulate the first-order observer at those values,
and report every rung against \emph{that} null rather than against $0.5$. Parameter uncertainty is
propagated: $d'$ and $c$ are re-estimated on each bootstrap resample, because simulating at the point
estimate gives a null that is too narrow and makes ``above null'' anticonservative. Each rung
receives a percentile against the null distribution, and those percentiles enter the same
Benjamini--Hochberg family as every other contrast.

\subsection{The verdict-leakage floor}
\label{sec:verdictfloor}

The second floor is specific to this target. Within the stratum $\texttt{gt}=A$, an item is correct
if and only if the judge said $A$. So a feature decoding only $\texttt{pred}$ is a perfect
within-stratum predictor. What prevents a pooled model from selecting it is sign cancellation: such
a feature must score $1.0$ in one stratum and $0.0$ in the other.

\textbf{Cancellation is exact only when the strata are balanced}, and judges are biased --- verdict
bias runs from $0.14$ to $0.64$ across our pilot panel. We therefore measure the residual directly:
a synthetic feature that decodes the judge's verdict and nothing else, pushed through the full
pipeline at each slice's own observed imbalance. The conditional AUROC it reaches is that slice's
leakage floor, and it differs from the SDT floor. A rung must clear both.

\section{Two leakage channels}
\label{sec:leakage}

\subsection{Item identity}
\label{sec:identity}

Because $y = (\texttt{pred}=\texttt{gt})$ and every judge has some verdict bias, $y$ is largely
determined by $\texttt{gt}$ alone. Any feature separating a positive item from a negative one
therefore scores without carrying a bit about self-knowledge, and spectral and activation features
do so trivially: the two prompts contain different text. We report
\texttt{identity\_decodability} --- how well the same features predict $\texttt{gt}$ --- as a
standing control. It runs \IdentLo{}--\IdentHi{} across our slices and, importantly, is
\emph{flat across judges of very different competence}: it is a property of the benchmark, not of the
model being measured, so it contaminates every result reported on that benchmark and cannot be
escaped by choosing a better judge. Conditioning on $\texttt{gt}$ removes it.

\subsection{Self-verdict}
\label{sec:verdict}

The twin channel is not removed by conditioning, because the quantity decoded is $\texttt{pred}$,
not $\texttt{gt}$ (\cref{sec:verdictfloor}). The last-token hidden state decodes $\texttt{pred}$
essentially perfectly --- it is the state the verdict logit is read from --- and we report
\texttt{verdict\_decodability} beside the identity control for exactly that reason.

The protection is the \emph{pooled fit}, which is a property of how the model is estimated rather
than of the metric. Fitting within stratum --- an innocuous-looking refactor --- removes it and
yields a near-perfect, entirely meaningless result. On planted data where a feature decodes
$\texttt{pred}$ and nothing else, the pooled fit lands near $0.5$ and a within-stratum fit exceeds
\PooledFitGuardWithin{}. We encode this as a regression test rather than a comment.

Leakage into type-2 AUROC is therefore two-dimensional: item identity, removed by conditioning, and
self-verdict, removed only by pooled estimation at balanced strata. Probes controlling for neither
are exposed to both.

\section{The ladder does not measure addition}
\label{sec:ladder}

A nested comparison is supposed to answer: what does this block add to what we already had? Fitting
it by concatenating the block onto the baseline columns under one shared penalty does not answer
that question when the two differ in width by three orders of magnitude. A \texttt{hidden\_size}-wide
activation block against a six-column baseline yields approximately ``activations'', not
``baseline $+$ activations'', and the block must reconstruct the margin from scratch. When it cannot,
the rung scores \emph{below} the baseline it was meant to extend.

\centering\small
\caption{Planted validation. The added block is \textbf{pure noise}; the correct delta is zero at
every width. Concatenation destroys real signal, and destroys more of it when there is more to
destroy.}
\label{tab:swamp}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
baseline & added block & concat & offset \\
\midrule
\SwampBaseStrong{} & \AddBlockNarrow{} noise cols & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$-$\SwampLossNarrow{}} & $\pm$\OffsetLossWide{} \\
\SwampBaseStrong{} & \AddBlockWide{} noise cols   & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$\mathbf{-\SwampLossWide{}}$} & $\pm$\OffsetLossWide{} \\
\SwampBaseWeak{}   & \AddBlockWide{} noise cols   & \multicolumn{1}{c}{$-$\SwampLossWeakWide{}} & $\pm$\OffsetLossWide{} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The signature is diagnostic: the delta correlates negatively with baseline strength, so the better
the baseline, the more the rung appears to lose. We observed exactly that on real data before
identifying the cause.

\paragraph{The offset estimator.} Fit the baseline first, carry its out-of-fold logit as a fixed
offset, and let the internal block fit only the working residual under ridge shrinkage:
$\text{score} = z_{\text{baseline}} + f(X_{\text{internal}})$. If the block is uninformative, $f$
shrinks to zero and the score reduces to the baseline, so addition is monotone by construction and a
negative delta means overfitting rather than destruction of information already in hand. The offset
for training rows comes from an \emph{inner} cross-validation inside the training fold; using the
outer out-of-fold logit would leak the test fold into the baseline the second stage sees.

\paragraph{Two estimands, reported side by side.} The offset rung answers ``what does this block add
to what we already had''. A block-only rung answers ``what is achievable from this block alone''.
The gap between them is redundancy. Both belong in the table: the offset alone reads as
self-handicapping, and the block-only alone hides that the information was already present.

\subsection{Absence versus redundancy}
\label{sec:absence}

A tight equivalence between $M_4$ and $M_3$ has three explanations the delta cannot separate:
spectral carries nothing; spectral carries what activations already carry; or the penalty shrank the
spectral block to zero. A fourth is not hypothetical (\cref{sec:hygiene}): the features never
arrived. We separate them with a block-only rung, the fitted coefficient norm on the spectral block
against the activation block, and a positive control that recovers a target known to be encoded at
matched $n$. On planted data the two worlds give indistinguishable deltas
(\AbsenceDeltaNoise{} and \AbsenceDeltaDup{}) and block-only rungs of \AbsenceNoiseOnly{} versus
\AbsenceDupOnly{}.

\section{Inference}
\label{sec:inference}

\paragraph{Clustering.} The pos/neg partners of one question and the two orders of one pairwise item
share nearly all their text and live in one cross-validation group. Paired DeLong assumes
independent items. On planted data with one item per group its standard error matches the grouped
bootstrap exactly (\DelongSEIndep{} both); with four clustered rows per question it understates the
standard error by \DelongRatioClustered{}$\times$. Since pairwise judge benchmarks are clustered by
construction, adopting DeLong as the primary test manufactures significance. We use a grouped paired
bootstrap, which is already paired --- both metrics are recomputed on identical resamples --- and
report DeLong only as a labelled cross-check whose ratio to the bootstrap measures how much width is
clustering.

\paragraph{Equivalence.} A non-significant contrast is not a finding unless it says what it rules
out. Every contrast carries a two-one-sided-test verdict against a pre-specified band of
\EqBand{} conditional AUROC, read off the same bootstrap so the grouping is respected.

\paragraph{Power.} The width of each bootstrap CI is the standard error at the pilot's $n$, so the
pilot prices its own minimum detectable effect. At \PilotSliceN{} items per slice and a median
standard error of \MedianSE{}, the detectable effect is \MdeUncorrected{} uncorrected and
\MdeFDR{} under Benjamini--Hochberg over \FamilySize{} contrasts. Against a plausible target of
\TargetEffect{}, a pilot null is uninformative, and detecting it requires roughly \NNeededFDR{} items
per slice. Two consequences shape the design: buy items rather than judges, since each judge
multiplies the contrast family without adding per-test power; and pre-specify \NPrimaryTests{}
primary tests, with everything else exploratory and unstarred.

\section{Numerical and provenance hygiene}
\label{sec:hygiene}

Two failures in this class nearly entered our own write-up as findings, and both are silent.

\paragraph{Precision.} Under \texttt{float16}, one judge overflowed to \texttt{inf} inside attention.
Its spectral analysis failed on 100\% of items --- caught per item by design, so nothing crashed and
no summary looked wrong --- \emph{and} its verdicts were corrupted, presenting it as a fully
degenerate judge (\NumFpAcc{} accuracy, \NumFpBias{} verdict bias) where under \texttt{bfloat16} it
is competent (\NumBfAcc{}). The two precisions agree on \NumAgreeFpBf{} of its verdicts. A numerical
bug was on its way into a write-up as a finding about model scale. Precision must be uniform across a
panel, since neither spectral metrics nor logits are comparable across dtypes. We have promoted the
check into the analysis library itself, which now raises on the first layer of non-finite attention.

\paragraph{Versions.} The pilot ran a different version of the spectral library than the environment
later held, and nothing in the results files recorded it. Comparing rows computed by different
library versions is the same class of error as mixing dtypes. We verified that the change on the
code path in use shifts every metric by at most \VerDtypeShift{} and pinned the version.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Named hazards we did not measure, each with the measurement we would propose.

\textbf{Chain-of-thought.} Our judges are single-token logprob readouts, which is what production
reward pipelines do but is not how a judge is always deployed. The signal-detection null transfers
unchanged --- it needs only a two-way argmax over a scalar margin at the readout token --- but
generated chain length correlates with item difficulty and would need to enter the baseline as a
nuisance covariate, exactly as prompt length does. We predict internals add \emph{less} under
chain-of-thought, because the model can externalise uncertainty into tokens the readout state has
already absorbed.

\textbf{Open-weight panel.} Internal access requires open weights, so the judges most used in
practice are absent.

\textbf{\texttt{single}-format label noise.} On preference datasets the ``is this response correct''
framing treats a relative preference as absolute truth, capping achievable accuracy. Pairwise is our
primary format for this reason, and is additionally immune to the length confound by
counterbalancing.

\textbf{Fixed spectral summarisation.} Three numbers per metric, chosen \emph{a priori} to avoid
post-hoc layer selection. If the signal is layer-local, this design cannot see it, and a null would
be about the summarisation rather than the representation. One pre-registered contrast on the full
layerwise vector addresses this at the cost of a single test.

\section{Corrections}
\label{sec:corrections}

We report our own errors grouped by cause, because the discipline this paper argues for is
worthless if applied only to others.

\textbf{Two artefacts that survived multiple-comparison correction.} A judge answering one way
almost always has near-zero within-stratum outcome variance; its conditional AUROC was estimated from
a stratum holding a single minority-class item, and a bootstrap resampling that same item returned a
\emph{narrow} interval around an arbitrary value. Two such contrasts passed
Benjamini--Hochberg and were briefly reported as discoveries. Fixed in three layers: a minimum class
count per stratum, contrast suppression for degenerate slices, and exclusion of unreliable slices
from the correction family.

\textbf{A validation that could not detect the failure it was for.} Our first planted test of the
ladder used a 32-column block and reported it clean. At that width the pathology of
\cref{sec:ladder} is \SwampLossNarrow{} and invisible; at realistic width it is \SwampLossWide{}.

\textbf{A trend we nearly reported.} The margin's conditional AUROC rose with judge competence
across our panel, which reads as self-knowledge emerging with capability. Simulating the
first-order observer of \cref{sec:estimand} reproduces the entire trend, and our judges sit at or
below it. The finding was arithmetic.
